About this app

It's hard to know what to believe about the ecological impact of generative AI when the Internet seems evenly divided between "Stop worrying" and "It's the apocalypse." This nonpartisan calculator is an attempt to provide a more objective comparison of the energy and water usage of various AI tasks versus other common digital activities, like watching Netflix or searching Google. The calculations are based on academic papers, industry reports, and expert estimates.

The calculations include only current energy and water usage, and not growth scenarios or actual environmental impact. Even for energy and water, it's extremely difficult to do more than estimate values given the lack of transparency in the tech industry. It's also unclear how some future tradeoffs will play out, eg whether improved efficiencies will cancel out increased demand. These calculations also exclude numerous potential AI downsides apart from environmental risks, which you can find explained in the IMPACT RISK framework.

You can find a list of all sources with rationales for calculations here. Please contact me with suggestions of research that updates or contradicts these findings.

These are the default units for energy and water, chosen because they are intuitive:

  • 💡lightbulb-minute (running an incandescent light bulb for 1 minute, equivalent to a watt-hour)
  • 🥤liter (about a quart)
  • ☔️cubic centimeter (or mL, a big raindrop).

This app is a project of the Learn with AI initiative, which aims to help educators and students understand the impact of AI on society. It is not affiliated with any commercial entity or political agenda.

How to use this in the classroom

First, thank you for caring about climate and education! In the hopes that this tool may help that cause, here are some recommendations for your students:

Quick exercises
  • Guess what's higher: Pick two tasks from the menus and predict which one uses more energy or water before clicking the Calculate button, then explain what you got right or wrong.
  • Find equivalences: Tweak the parameters until two tasks have the same footpring, eg "How many seconds of AI video generated from a text prompt equal the energy of watching a 1-hour Netflix movie?"
Longer exercises
  • Audit your energy diet: Estimate the hours you spent last week on AI versus other digital activities like social media, education, and entertainment, or pull real data from your iPhone, Android phone, MacOS, or Windows. Plot one pie chart showing the relative time for each category of activity. Then use What Uses More to calculate the corresponding energy or water usage for each and plot a second pie chart showing the relative footprint for each category. How do the two charts compare?

    Note that you may need to activate Screen Time on your device for a full week to get accurate data.

    (For extra points, research the percentage of social media that is currently AI-generated and factor that into your charts.)

  • Make a personal AI budget: Draft a budget for your own responsible use of AI. Will you use it for professional or personal purposes? For generating text, images, or video? Will you limit your other digital or non-digital activities to compensate?
  • Check sources: Research and present on the sources used in this calculator, evaluating their reliability and relevance.
  • Expand the activity list: Brainstorm other digital activities not included in this tool and estimate their energy and water usage based on similar calculations.
  • Use statistics for good and evil: Create two infographics, one that could mislead the public using out-of-context comparisons, and one that responsibly conextualizes impact.
  • Probe inequities: Set two set of conditions, one to represent an affluent community and another a poor community (eg, a drought-risk state or city with a vulnerable electrical grid) and compare AI's relative impact on local power or water.
Do's
  • Do encourage students to experiment with different combinations of tasks, energy sources (solar vs coal), task depth, and local climate to see how these factors affect energy and water usage.
  • Do brainstorm what environmental impacts are missing from these parameters, such as energy embedded in server components, data center strains on local electricity and water supplies, or the health risks of discarded electronics (e-waste).
  • Do remind students that these figures are estimates based on incomplete and often contradictory sources.
  • Do let me know any other activities or suggestions to share!
Don'ts
  • Don't use this tool to make definitive claims about the environmental impact of AI. The calculations are based on estimates and assumptions that may be superseded in future.
  • Don't ignore the broader context of AI's environmental impact, including hardware manufacturing, data center cooling, and other factors not covered by this calculator.
  • Don't permit students (say, above middle school) to cite this tool directly; require them instead to track down and read the related sources in the associated Google Sheet.
